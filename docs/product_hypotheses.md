# Product hypotheses

This document summarises possible product hypotheses based on the funnel and cohort analysis.

## 1. Reduce the view-to-cart drop-off

If the largest drop-off is between product view and add-to-cart, the product card or product page may not communicate enough value.

Possible actions:
- improve product title and main image;
- make price and delivery information more visible;
- strengthen the add-to-cart button;
- show similar items or recommendations.

Expected metric:
- increase in view-to-cart conversion.

## 2. Improve cart-to-purchase conversion

If many users add products to cart but do not complete a transaction, the checkout flow may contain friction.

Possible actions:
- simplify checkout steps;
- show delivery cost earlier;
- reduce mandatory fields;
- add reminders for abandoned carts.

Expected metric:
- increase in cart-to-purchase conversion.

## 3. Improve retention of new cohorts

If retention after the first month is low, users may not have enough reasons to return.

Possible actions:
- personalised recommendations;
- email or push reminders;
- discounts for second purchase;
- better onboarding after first visit.

Expected metric:
- increase in month-1 retention.

## 4. Suggested A/B-test idea

Test a redesigned product page with a clearer add-to-cart button and improved delivery information.

Control group:
- current product page.

Treatment group:
- redesigned product page.

Primary metric:
- view-to-cart conversion.

Secondary metrics:
- transaction conversion;
- revenue per visitor;
- bounce rate from product page.

Guardrail metrics:
- page load time;
- return rate;
- checkout errors.
